# General All Policies — Risk Mitigation Factor Checklist

This checklist is the floor for audit completeness. Every item must be evaluated for every policy in the program. Items found compliant become Good findings; defects become Bad or Ugly; ambiguous items requiring human judgment become Needs Review; items genuinely not applicable to this client (e.g., no foreign operations means no DBA needed) are omitted silently.

---

## 1. 90-day notice of cancellation

90-day notice of cancellation is the broker benchmark. Verify the cancellation provision on every policy:
- For-cause and non-payment notice periods
- Whether endorsements (e.g., 401-1235 Designated Entity NOC for Hanover) extend the notice period
- Whether designated customers/counties are listed on cancellation-notice schedules per contract requirements

Anything less than the contract requirement is a Bad or Ugly finding (depending on severity of the contract relationship). Anything less than 60 days without contract requirements is worth flagging. The broker target is 90 days.

## 2. Unintentional Errors and Omissions

Does the policy include an Unintentional Errors and Omissions giveback (often endorsed for GL — sometimes embedded in package forms)? Without it, an inadvertent failure to report a location, vehicle, or operation can result in coverage denial. Standard-market carriers commonly offer this as a no-cost or low-cost endorsement.

Often appears as a giveback embedded in commercial package forms rather than a separate endorsement — check both endorsement schedules AND form definitions.

## 3. Named insureds — list for each policy and flag any missing

For each policy, capture the full Named Insured + Additional Named Insured list. Compare across the program — if an entity (subsidiary, JV, holding company, real-estate LLC, 401(k) plan) appears on some policies but not others, flag the asymmetry. Pay special attention to:
- Inc vs LLC entity-type mismatches on the Dec page
- Broad Form Named Insured endorsements that may exclude LLCs/partnerships from automatic extension
- Entities scheduled on Auto/Pro Liab but missing from Umbrella (the most common gap)

## 4. Notice and Knowledge of Claims (all policies except umbrella)

Is the policy endorsed so that a covered "occurrence" is only triggered when the company's officer, the person responsible for insurance, the risk manager, or equivalent is notified — rather than ANY employee's knowledge being imputed to the entity? Without this endorsement, a low-level employee's unreported incident can void coverage. Common endorsement names: "Notice Manager," "Knowledge of Occurrence," "Imputation of Knowledge."

(Umbrella is excluded because umbrella follows form on this — the underlying policies' notice-and-knowledge endorsements flow up. Verify the endorsement is on each underlying policy, not on the umbrella itself.)

## 5. Waiver of Subrogation

Is a waiver of subrogation in place — and is it BLANKET (any party where required by written contract) rather than scheduled? Verify that:
- The waiver applies on every policy where the contract requires it (typically GL, Auto, WC, Property, Inland Marine — usually NOT on Professional Liability / E&O). Note WC may require state-specific WoS endorsement forms.
- The waiver wording is "best practices" (no narrow carve-outs)
- Cross-policy: WoS should be consistent across the program — flag if it's blanket on some policies but scheduled or absent on others
