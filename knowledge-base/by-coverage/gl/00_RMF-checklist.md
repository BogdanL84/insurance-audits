# General Liability — Risk Mitigation Factor Checklist

This checklist contains every item to verify when auditing a General Liability policy. For each item: find the relevant policy language, note the page number, and assess whether it is Good, Bad, Ugly, or needs verification.

---

## 1. Additional Insured: Does the policy include blanket AI endor

Additional Insured: Does the policy include blanket AI endorsement? Is the AI form utilizing the words "arising from" and does the form or forms include ongoing operations and products or completed operations? Does the AI form contain any exclusions or negative conditions?

## 2. Care Custody and Control

Care Custody and Control - Does your Insured have or could they have property of others in their care custody and/or control? (e.g., A machine shop is working on someone's equipment; a technology company is testing their software that runs a third party's equipment, or a contractor is installing equipment that belongs to a third party?)

## 3. Compliance: Are the insurance policies in compliance with ba

Compliance: Are the insurance policies in compliance with bank covenants, master deeds, contracts and/or leases? It is best to obtain copies of these documents and perform a review. This item is closely related to items 1 & 5 but more from the aspect of reviewing the contracts and making certain they are funded by the insurance policy.

## 4. Composite Rating, is there an opportunity to use composite r

Composite Rating, is there an opportunity to use composite rating to the benefit of the customer? You have an opportunity to be very creative with this criteria - accounts can be won on this single concept.

## 5. Contract Review procedure? Remember contracts include purcha

Contract Review procedure? Remember contracts include purchase orders and invoice Terms and Conditions.

## 6. Contractual Liability

Contractual Liability - Part 1 Does the policy have the proper wording for an Insured contract and if so has it been altered, typically (ISO forms) with the amendment of insured contract definition? As part 2, is the policy set up to anticipate, additional insured, primary and non-contributory and waiver of subrogation contract requirements automatically triggered by written contract (blanket)? Note relative to part 1 - the standard ISO form includes contractual liability - it is only problematic when the Iso form, CG 00 01 is endorsed, typically with the amendment of insured contract definition.

## 7. Contractual liability for personal and advertising injury -h

Contractual liability for personal and advertising injury -have we endorsed this coverage back on to the policy?

## 8. Coverage Territory

Coverage Territory - Is the insured's product sold outside the USA or Canada or "could" their product end up outside the US territories/possessions, or Canada?

## 9. Cyber Coverage

Cyber Coverage - first party and third party. If this is endorsed on the CGL, have coalition help you with a coverage analysis - the GL endorsements for Cyber pale in comparison to a stand alone form.

## 10. Damage to premises

Damage to premises - You must calculate the prospect's exposure versus the policy sub-limit.

## 11. Damages to Premises

Damages to Premises - (Fire Legal Liability) damage to premises extension to include other perils.

## 12. Deductibles

Deductibles - What are any and all deductibles and do they make sense?

## 13. Duty to Defend

Duty to Defend - Do all policies include the duty to defend clause (most will, D&O/EPL will vary)? Secondarily - Is it important for the Insured to be able to pick their own attorney? If so we must negotiate this point before we place cover.

## 14. Employer Benefit Liability (EBL) Coverage

Employer Benefit Liability (EBL) Coverage - Does the insured have EBL coverage?  Is there a retro date issue?

## 15. Employment Practices Liability Insurance

Employment Practices Liability Insurance - is EPLI included, what perils does the definition of wrongful acts include?  Can you select your own attorney? PAY SPECIAL ATTENTION TO COVERAGE FOR WAGE AND HOUR CLAIMS - at a minimum defense coverage only.

## 16. Endorsements

Endorsements - List every endorsement under the "why" column. Highlight the problematic endorsements in red and insert a comment as for the explanation as well as inserting the page number. Use the top 50 most horrible endorsement form to help you. The operative word in the preceding sentence is "help" you must read the endorsement, critical.

## 17. Environmental, does the prospect/insured purchase environmen

Environmental, does the prospect/insured purchase environmental coverage/exposure?  If they do, what's covered and is the limit appropriate?

## 18. Errors & Omissions

Errors & Omissions - 
A professional type insurance that protects companies and individuals against claims made by clients for defective work and/or negligent actions. GCL coverage is triggered by bodily injury or property damage to a third patty caused by an occurrence. Thus if there is no BI or PD there is no coverage. E&O is triggered by a wrongful act, such as an error in the design or fabrication of a product. Coverage is mostly considered for the construction and manufacturing industry, although coverage is not limited to these industries.

## 19. Faulty Work

Faulty Work - Does the policy provide the giveback for subcontractors work?

## 20. Fellow Employee Coverage

Fellow Employee Coverage - Is the Fellow Employee Exclusion deleted?

## 21. Hired and Non-owned Coverage

Hired and Non-owned Coverage - is this included on the CGL as needed, when the insured does not have an auto policy. Does the insured have a WC ghost policy exposure?

## 22. Leased workers and temporary workers

Leased workers and temporary workers - does or could the insured lease workers ? And if so, have we requested the Lessor to add the alternate employers endorsement to their policy and or deleted leased workers as an employee on our insured's CGL form?

## 23. Mental Anguish

Mental Anguish - Is it included in the definition of Bodily Injury? We want mental anguish in and of itself - we don't want it connected to bodily injury.

## 24. Named Insured

Named Insured - It is best to see the insured's financial report in order to discover all entities.  You may also want to look at an org. chart.  Does the policy have broad wording to include all subsidiaries? Joint Ventures - Is the insured involved in any joint ventures, teaming agreements and or partnerships  and if so are they properly listed on the policy?

## 25. Notice & Knowledge

Notice & Knowledge - Is the policy wording endorsed so notice of an occurrence is only considered when an officer, the person responsible for insurance, and/or the risk manager is notified?

## 26. Per Location  and or Per Project Aggregate

Per Location  and or Per Project Aggregate - does the insured have this endorsement?

## 27. Primary and Non-Contributory

Primary and Non-Contributory - Is primary and non-contributory in favor of the AI included within the Additional Insured form or on a separate endorsement and is the wording best practices?

## 28. Product Recall

Product Recall - does the insured have this coverage?

## 29. Professional Coverage -A type of liability coverage designed

Professional Coverage -A type of liability coverage designed to protect traditional professionals (e.g., accountants, attorneys) and quasi-professionals (e.g., real estate brokers, consultants) against liability incurred as a result of errors and omissions in performing their professional services. Although there are a few exceptions (e.g., physicians, architects, and engineers), most professional liability policies only cover economic or financial losses suffered by third parties, as opposed to bodily injury (BI) and property damage (PD) claims. This is because the latter two types of loss are typically covered under commercial general liability (CGL) policies. The vast majority of professional liability policies are written with claims-made coverage triggers. In addition, professional liability policies contain what are known as "shrinking limits," meaning that unlike CGL policies (where defense costs are paid in addition to policy limits), the insurer's payment of defense costs reduces available policy limits. Accordingly, when attempting to determine appropriate policy limits, insureds must consider the fact that because defense costs are often a high proportion of any claim settlement or judgment, they must usually purchase additional limits. The most common exclusions in professional liability policy forms are for BI, PD, and intentional/dishonest acts.

## 30. Stop Gap

Stop Gap - does the prospect have coverage under the CGL or WC? Do they have the exposure or could they have it regardless of their location?

## 31. Total Pollution Exclusion

Total Pollution Exclusion - is this exclusion endorsed on to the policy?

## 32. Tria

Tria - Does the insured buy terrorism and has it been explained thoroughly.

## 33. Waiver of subrogation

Waiver of subrogation - does the policy have the correct wording and is it blanket?
